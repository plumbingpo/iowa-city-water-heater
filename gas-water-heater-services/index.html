No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Here in Iowa City, IA, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Iowa City Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Iowa City call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. Call Iowa City Water Heater at (319) 422-8381 at the first sign of trouble.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. Guess wrong and you have spent money without fixing anything. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically call for a permit.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
