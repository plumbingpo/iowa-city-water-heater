How Solon Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Solon

A water heater in Solon almost never fails out of nowhere. Pay attention to rumbling and popping while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or showers that run cold sooner every month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
