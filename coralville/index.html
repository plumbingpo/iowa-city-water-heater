How Coralville Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
