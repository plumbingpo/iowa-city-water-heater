If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Iowa City, IA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

The work is hands-on and specific. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Maintenance

Not every maintenance task is a good DIY project. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are another story. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
