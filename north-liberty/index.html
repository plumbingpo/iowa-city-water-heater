How North Liberty Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in North Liberty

In North Liberty, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks lose heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
